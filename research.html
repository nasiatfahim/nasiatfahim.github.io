Research

What breaks when a model leaves home

My projects use different scientific data, but they ask the same question: when, where, and why does a model stop generalising?

Research vision

Reliability beyond the training distribution

Scientific machine-learning systems rarely receive data that perfectly resembles their training set. Sensors change, regions differ, labels remain limited, and biological or physical regimes can shift the underlying distribution.

High performance in distribution therefore does not guarantee useful behaviour elsewhere. I study generalisation failure as a scientific object: which conditions cause it, whether uncertainty or attribution can expose it, and how adaptation can improve reliability without hiding new weaknesses.

Protein sequences, satellite radar, and seismic waveforms provide different tests of this idea. Across all three, my goal is to build models whose predictions can be evaluated together with the conditions under which they should be believed.

Adapting protein language models to thermophile sequence space

Research question
What does a protein language model lose by averaging across organisms with very different environmental adaptations?
Challenge
Large protein models mix many lifestyles and thermal regimes into one training distribution. Apparent improvements can also come from near-duplicate sequences rather than genuine adaptation.
Approach
I adapt ProGen2 using LoRA-based supervised fine-tuning, DPO, and KTO under matched compute budgets and homology-controlled evaluation.
Contribution
My work compares likelihood and preference objectives directly and examines their distinct trade-offs instead of treating all adaptation objectives as equivalent.
Current direction
I use attribution methods to study which residue, charge, and sequence patterns the adapted models associate with thermophile biology.

Cross-regional water segmentation from Sentinel-1 SAR

Research question
How far can a water-segmentation model move across regions and biomes before its predictions become unreliable?
Challenge
SAR backscatter changes with wind, incidence angle, vegetation, terrain, speckle, shadow, and layover. Labels are limited, and geographic validation can hide local failure modes.
Approach
I train and compare cross-regional segmentation models, evaluate geographic and biome shift systematically, and inspect model behaviour with explainability methods.
Contribution
My work centres the conditions of failure rather than only mean segmentation performance, connecting geographic variation with prediction reliability.
Current direction
I am developing a controlled evaluation framework for cross-region generalisation, interpretability, and reliable Sentinel-1-only water mapping.

Current research 03

Earthquake early warning

Published + ongoing

IEEE ICCIT 2025
Extension in progress

Early inference from initial P-waves

Research question
What earthquake information can be inferred reliably from the first seven seconds of one station's P-wave, and how well does that inference transfer across regions?
Challenge
An early-warning system must make a decision before the damaging S-wave arrives. The data are imbalanced, stations and instruments vary, and a regional model can learn local artefacts.
Approach
I curated 7,318 South Asian events and compared logistic regression, gradient boosting, CNN, residual CNN, BiLSTM, and Transformer models under a shared protocol.
Contribution
The study established a regional evaluation dataset and a six-model comparison. The Transformer reached 76.23% accuracy, 81.56% adaptive accuracy, and 4.8 ms inference latency.
Current direction
I am extending the work toward cross-region transfer and relationships between early waveforms, shaking intensity, radiated energy, and source properties, with explainability-based checks.

Protein-based fish species identification

I curated 2,845 protein sequences from nine native Bangladeshi fish species and compared seven architectures. A motif-aware CNN-Transformer with type-aware positional encoding achieved 79.80% accuracy while remaining suitable for CPU inference.

Future direction

Where I want to take this

I want to move from simply optimising predictive performance toward understanding when, where, and why machine-learning systems fail under distribution shift.

My emerging direction combines domain generalisation, robustness, uncertainty, calibration, explainability, adaptation, model self-assessment, and efficient scientific foundation models. I am especially interested in methods that report not only a prediction, but also evidence about whether that prediction should be trusted.

I want to test these ideas on scientific domains where the ground truth is physical and failure matters outside the benchmark, while keeping the methods broad enough to transfer across biological, Earth, and engineering data.
