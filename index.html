De-averaging protein language models

A protein language model is trained across all of life at once. What does it lose by averaging over organisms that live at 4 °C and at 95 °C alike?

I adapt ProGen2 toward thermophile sequence space using LoRA-based supervised fine-tuning together with DPO and KTO preference optimisation, under matched compute and homology-controlled evaluation. The interesting result so far is a clean separation between likelihood and preference objectives — they do not want the same thing. Attribution methods are then used to read back the biophysical adaptation rules the adapted model has actually learned.

2025 –

Where does a water-segmentation model stop working?

Sentinel‑1 flood-mapping models are almost always validated in the biome they were trained in. How far can one actually travel before it fails?

I build cross-regional Sentinel‑1 water-segmentation models and then try to break them on purpose — measuring geographic and biome bias, backscatter variability, speckle, and radar shadow and layover effects. The goal is a characterisation of failure modes, not another leaderboard number: knowing where a flood map is untrustworthy matters more, operationally, than the mean IoU.

Extension in progress

How much can seven seconds tell you?

Early-warning systems must commit to an answer from a fragment of one waveform at one station. What is genuinely recoverable from that fragment, and where does it break across regions?

I work on globally generalisable early-warning models from initial P‑waves, linking waveform character to shaking, radiated energy and source properties. My published work curated 7,318 South Asian events and benchmarked six architectures; the current extension is about cross-region transfer and using explainability to check that the model is reading physics rather than station artefacts.
